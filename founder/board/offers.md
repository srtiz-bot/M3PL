# Offers lens
Score: 5/10 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong
- **Low effort to start.** Marcana does the implementation and store connections, and the 14-day trial waives fulfillment fees. The buyer risks little to try it.
- **Fast result.** Same-day shipping and a one-business-day reply promise are concrete and easy to understand.
- **Clear, published price.** $149 a month plus $1.95 an order, with a calculator. Most buyers can price themselves without a call.
- **Real pain.** A seller packing their own orders, or let down by their current warehouse, feels it daily.
- **Bilingual support** is a genuine point of difference for Spanish-speaking sellers. It is the only one in the brief that is not price or speed.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)
1. **It reads as a commodity sold on price.** Pick, pack, same-day shipping and "no hidden fees" are the whole pitch. Nothing in the brief says why a seller should pick Marcana over any other warehouse except that it looks cheap and simple. A cheaper competitor takes that away overnight.
2. **No proof and no guarantee.** The brief shows no client count, no results, no testimonials, and no promise about accuracy or on-time shipping. Handing over all your inventory is a high-trust decision. A free trial lowers the cost of trying, not the fear of lost or mis-shipped stock.
3. **The offer targets buyers with little money.** The quote form welcomes "Startup" sellers still setting up and under-100-shipment accounts. At 100 orders a month a client pays $344. With no published storage, receiving or setup fee, small accounts may cost more to serve than they pay. Cost per order is unknown, so this cannot be ruled out.

## The questions I would ask the founder
- Which of the four stages and volume bands make you money, and which lose it?
- What does an order cost you to fulfill, all in?
- With no storage fee, what stops a client parking slow stock for $149 a month?
- What share of trials become paying clients, and how long do they stay?
- What is your order accuracy and same-day ship rate, measured? Would you put money behind it?
- Why do clients leave their last warehouse, and why do they leave you?
- If you doubled the price tomorrow, who would stay, and why?
- Six services from one warehouse: which one do clients actually buy?

## If FUND IF: the conditions
1. **Prove unit economics.** Show cost per order and margin per client by volume band. Set a minimum volume or a storage fee where small accounts lose money.
2. **Add a guarantee backed by measured numbers.** For example, a credit for any order shipped late or wrong. Only promise what the data supports.
3. **Publish proof.** Real client counts, accuracy and ship-time figures, and named client results.
4. **Pick one buyer and name the offer for them.** One stage, one or two industries, and a stack built around that buyer's specific problems, so the choice is not made on price.
