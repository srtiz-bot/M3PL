# Monopoly lens
Score: 3 | Vote: FUND IF

## What is strong

- A possible small market to own: Spanish-speaking e-commerce sellers who need a US warehouse. Bilingual support and Spanish service pages point at it. Miami fits it.
- Distribution exists: a page per service in two languages, a calculator, a quote form, WhatsApp. Whether it works is unknown.
- The founder has sold online for 10+ years, so knows the buyer.
- Published flat pricing and a 14-day trial lower the cost of trying it.

## What would kill it (the top 3, most dangerous first)

1. **It is a commodity.** Nothing in the brief is ten times better than another warehouse. Receive, store, pick, pack, ship is incremental. The software is ShipHero, which any rival can license. No proprietary technology, no network effect, no scale, no known brand. A copycat can match the whole offer in month six with a lease and the same software. That means competing on price.
2. **No small market is being dominated.** Six services, four customer stages, four industries, under 100 to 5,000+ orders a month, US and international. That is everyone, from one warehouse open eight hours on weekdays. The one defensible niche, bilingual sellers, is a feature on the site, not the business.
3. **The price may be the only reason to choose it, and may not pay.** $149 plus $1.95 an order, with no storage, receiving or setup fee and carrier at cost. Cost per order and rent are unknown. If the published price is below what it costs to serve small accounts, growth makes it worse, and the "Startup" stage invites clients with no orders.

## The questions I would ask the founder

- Who else sells fulfillment to the same buyer, in Miami and online? The brief names none.
- What share of clients are Spanish-speaking or Latin America based? Is that the market to own?
- What is the secret: what do you know about these sellers that other warehouses do not?
- How did the last 20 clients arrive, and what did each cost to win?
- How many clients, how many orders a month, what churn?
- What does an order cost you, all in? Which volume band makes money?
- Why now?
- What does a client lose by leaving? How long does switching take?
- What will be true in ten years that a new warehouse cannot copy?

## If FUND IF: the conditions

1. Pick one small market and prove a lead in it. The obvious candidate is Spanish-speaking sellers entering the US through Miami. Show that a meaningful share of current clients already fit.
2. Show per-order profit on the published plan, by volume band, with rent and labour included.
3. Show client count, churn, and cost to win a client. If churn is high, there is no durability to fund.
4. Name one thing a copycat cannot match in six months. If the answer is service quality or price, the vote becomes PASS.
5. Cut the stages and services that do not serve the chosen market.
