# Board summary: Marcana 3PL

Reviewed from the public website only. No internal numbers were available, and
nothing was independently verified.

## The vote

**3 fund if, 0 fund, 0 pass. Average score 4.3 out of 10.**

| Lens | Score | Vote |

| Monopoly | 3 | Fund if |

The Monopoly member added that the vote becomes a pass if the only answer to
"what can a copycat not match in six months" is service quality or price.

## Risks more than one member raised

1. **It reads as a commodity (all three).** Pick, pack, same-day shipping and
   "no hidden fees" are the whole pitch. The software is ShipHero, which any
   warehouse can license. Nothing on the site says why Marcana over another
   3PL except that it looks cheap and simple.
2. **It tries to serve everyone (all three).** Six services, four customer
   stages, four industries, under 100 to 5,000+ orders a month, from one
   warehouse open weekdays 9:30 to 5:30. The pitch needs a paragraph.
3. **The price may not pay on small accounts (Offers, Monopoly).** At 100
   orders a month a client pays $344. No storage, receiving or setup fee is
   published, and the "Startup" stage invites clients with no orders yet. Cost
   per order is unknown, so nobody can say which accounts make money.
4. **Promise without proof (Offers, Product).** No client count, accuracy
   rate, on-time rate, testimonials or guarantee. "Same-day shipping" has no
   stated cutoff time. Handing over all your inventory is a high-trust
   decision, and a free trial lowers the cost of trying, not the fear.

## Where the members disagreed

- **How bad the commodity problem is.** Monopoly scored it 3 and treats it as
  close to fatal without a niche. Offers and Product scored 5 and see it as
  fixable with a guarantee, proof and focus.
- **What to focus on.** Monopoly names the market: Spanish-speaking sellers
  entering the US through Miami. Offers and Product say pick one buyer and
  one lead service but do not say which.
- **The flat price.** Product calls the published price the clearest thing in
  the business and worth protecting. Offers and Monopoly worry it is the only
  reason to buy and may sit below cost for small clients.

## Conditions, merged

Numbers only the founder has:

- [ ] Client count, monthly orders, trial-to-paid conversion, churn
- [ ] Cost per order all in (labour, rent, materials), and margin per client by volume band — `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] How the last 20 clients arrived and what each cost to win
- [ ] Share of current clients who are Spanish-speaking or Latin America based
- [ ] Measured order accuracy and same-day ship rate
- [ ] Warehouse capacity before quality drops

Decisions:

- [ ] Pick one customer and one lead service; say it in one sentence on the home page; make the rest add-ons — `/founder-consumer`, `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Set a minimum volume or a storage fee wherever small accounts lose money — `/founder-pricing`, `/founder-cfo`
- [ ] Publish every fee (storage, receiving, returns), or state plainly there are none
- [ ] State the same-day cutoff time
- [ ] Add a guarantee backed by measured numbers, such as a credit for any late or wrong order — `/founder-offer`
- [ ] Publish proof: client counts, accuracy and ship-time figures, named client results
- [ ] Name one thing a competitor cannot copy in six months — `/founder-competitors`
- [ ] Write the first 14 days step by step, with one owner per handoff — `/founder-ops`

## The strongest version the board can see

This differs from what the site pitches today. The strongest version is not
"fulfillment for online stores of all sizes". It is the Miami fulfillment
partner for Spanish-speaking e-commerce founders selling into the US, fully
bilingual from the first WhatsApp message to the invoice, with one lead service,
a published price that includes every fee, a stated same-day cutoff, and a
money-backed guarantee on accuracy and speed. That is a small market a
one-warehouse operator can plausibly lead, and language and trust are harder
for a rival to copy than a price. Whether it is the right niche depends on a
number the board does not have: how many current clients already fit it.
